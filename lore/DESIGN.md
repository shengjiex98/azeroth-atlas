# Azeroth Atlas design

The map is the starting point for exploring Azeroth. The continent view leads to
individual zones, where lore covers local hubs, history, and storylines in story
order. Sagas, the glossary, and sources open from the map. Zone outlines provide
click and tap targets. The reading panel sits beside the map on desktop and
opens as a bottom sheet on phones.

The journey is an optional layer on the same maps. The continent view shows
visited zones. The zone view groups time spent, quest turn-ins, and deaths into
numbered places in first-visit order. Revisits keep their original number.
**Menu → Experiments** offers continent journey shading, quest counts, and visit
order, plus styles for city crests and visit markers. Lore stands on its own and
follows the story of each place independently of the character or any guide.

## Architecture

GitHub Pages serves `lore/` directly as static HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and images.
The atlas also opens from disk. `atlas/data/world.js` holds the lore.
`atlas/data/geo.js` holds continent geometry and zone outlines generated by
`tools/atlas_geo.py` from map bounds and the outlines traced by
`tools/atlas_shapes.py`. The geometry generator can use map data exported from
Rosetta when available. `atlas/data/ui_map_bounds.js` supplies world bounds for
clustering zone observations by distance.

Pages stamps the Atlas HTML and its local script and stylesheet URLs with the deploy commit, and publishes that commit in `atlas/version.json`.
Open tabs check for a new version on focus, when visible again, and every minute while visible, like Avalon. The automatic reload keeps the URL and waits for input, dialogs and map popovers to clear; the banner's Reload button reloads at once.
If a reload is deferred, the banner offers a Reload button and the next safe return to the tab reloads once per version in that session.

Rosetta records gameplay in the WoW: Forever client, including zone visits,
position samples, quest turn-ins, deaths, and level-ups, with time and XP totals
by map cell. With QuestieDB loaded, it fills missing completed quest turn-ins
with the finisher's location when available. Rosetta marks those turn-ins as
backfilled and leaves their timestamps empty. The route comes from recorded zone
visits and zones inferred from backfilled turn-ins.

`/rosetta export` produces a compact [journey summary](../Interface/AddOns/Rosetta/SUMMARY.md).
A pasted summary or a summary in the URL fragment is the sole source of a
character's journey in the atlas. `atlas/journey.js` decodes it and builds the
layer in the browser. `atlas/app.js` stores imported summaries per character in
browser storage and lets the player switch or remove them. A fresh import
updates the journey.

With a journey loaded, the reading panel opens on the latest session recap.
The recap links to the chapters touched in that session. Authored chain pages
pair chapter prose with turn-in map crops and keep the quest cards in
disclosures. The next chapter and the story's meaning unlock with progress.
See `journeySessions`, `sessionStories`, `chapterProgress`, and
`chapterInterludes` in [atlas/journey.js](atlas/journey.js) for the journal
rules, and `sessionRecap` and `chainStory` in [atlas/app.js](atlas/app.js) for
the reading views.
